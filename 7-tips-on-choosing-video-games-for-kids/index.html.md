# 7 Smart Tips on Choosing Video Games for Kids: A Parent's Guide

Playing video games is a popular entertainment activity for most young children today. A number of parents fear that allowing their child to indulge in gaming will take them away from better, more fulfilling activities like playing outdoors or socializing with friends. However, video games can actually help your child develop useful cognitive and social skills, including critical judgment, teamwork, problem-solving, and understanding that every choice carries consequences. For this reason, it is wise to permit gaming while selecting titles thoughtfully. Here are 7 practical tips on choosing video games for kids.

## 1. Check the Rating

![Check the Rating](https://resize.allw.mn/1028x0/filters:format(webp)/filters:quality(70)/content/parenting/2011/02/game_ratings_500x387.jpg)

Image source: [electronichouse.com](http://www.electronichouse.com/images/uploads/game_ratings.jpg)

Every video game features an official age rating assigned by the [Entertainment Software Rating Board (ESRB)](https://www.esrb.org). This rating gives buyers a clear summary of the type of content included, though it shouldn't be the only factor in your decision. The rating serves as a reliable baseline to quickly gauge content suitability for your child's age group.

| Rating Category | Target Age | Content Overview |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Everyone (E) | Ages 6 and up | Minimal cartoon violence, family-friendly |
| Everyone 10+ (E10+) | Ages 10 and up | Mild language, fantasy violence |
| Teen (T) | Ages 13 and up | Violence, crude humor, mild suggestive themes |

## 2. Read up on the Game

Apart from reviewing the rating board score, it is a great idea to research the specific games your child is requesting. While general box descriptions provide basic info, online parent reviews and trusted resources like [Common Sense Media](https://www.commonsensemedia.org) offer detailed breakdowns of themes, difficulty levels, and age-appropriateness. Before buying, it is also smart to [check the rating](#1) details online to read user feedback from other families.

## 3. Check Features of the Game

While researching whether a title includes coarse language or intense scenes, pay close attention to its interactive features. Many modern games include online multiplayer and open voice or text chat, which allows players to interact with strangers. If you want to limit unrestricted online interactions, checking these features in advance helps you set appropriate boundaries for your child.

- In-game voice and text chat channels

- Unfiltered multiplayer matchmaking

- In-app purchases and microtransactions

## 4. Learn about Video Games

![Learn about Video Games](https://resize.allw.mn/1028x0/filters:format(webp)/filters:quality(70)/content/parenting/2011/02/VIDEO-GAMES-articleLarge_500x347.jpg)

Image source: [graphics8.nytimes.com](http://graphics8.nytimes.com/images/2010/11/26/arts/VIDEO-GAMES/VIDEO-GAMES-articleLarge.jpg)

Taking time to learn about current video game trends and hardware gives you valuable perspective as a parent. Take the time to understand major gaming platforms, consoles, and controllers. Familiarize yourself with popular genres, and talk with fellow parents sharing similar experiences so you stay well-informed about what your kids are playing.

## 5. Play the Game Yourself

Once you bring a game home, try playing it yourself first to see its gameplay, storyline, and themes firsthand. Testing the game personally allows you to discover hidden features or unannounced content that might not be suitable for your child. Before handing over the controller, take a moment to [check features of the game](#3) and confirm they align with your family values.

## 6. Learn How to Use Parental Controls

While discovering new games and exploring console hardware, make sure you know how to set up built-in parental controls. Leading gaming platform providers offer robust tools to manage screen time, block mature content, and restrict digital store purchases. Take time to configure these settings on your child's device and review them regularly to ensure they remain active across all installed titles.

- Daily and weekly screen time limits

- Content filtering by ESRB rating tier

- PIN protection for digital store purchases

## 7. Observe Your Child

Finally, continue observing your child's mood and habits after introducing a new game. If you notice signs of irritability, emotional withdrawal, or mood swings when screen time ends, the game may not be a healthy fit. Paying attention to these subtle changes helps you decide whether to adjust playing privileges or explore alternative activities using [parental controls](#6) to maintain a balanced routine.

Allowing video games into your home is fine as long as you do so with your eyes wide open. There are a number of harmful games out there so with the help of the steps provided above, make educated decisions, which will serve you well.

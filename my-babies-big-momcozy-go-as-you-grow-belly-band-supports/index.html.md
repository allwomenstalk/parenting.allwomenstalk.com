## The Reality of Carrying Bigger Babies

Women carrying larger babies often experience more pressure earlier in pregnancy, and I certainly fit that description.

With my second son arriving only fifteen months after my first, everything happened faster.  He settled low almost immediately.  By five months, strangers were asking if I was due “any day now.”  I wasn’t.

✅The pelvic pressure was extraordinary. By late afternoon it genuinely felt as though labor might begin at any moment, even though months remained.

✅That low position also wreaked havoc on my posture. When your center of gravity shifts that dramatically, your lower back quickly becomes the unwilling hero of the entire operation.

✅Walking longer distances became increasingly difficult. Continuous activity disappeared from my vocabulary. I could probably draw an illustrated guide to every reasonably clean public restroom, shaded bench, and comfortable sitting area on Manhattan’s Upper West Side. Lincoln Center and Central Park deserve medals for their contributions to pregnant women everywhere.

✅Standing for long periods became equally challenging. Chopping vegetables? Sitting at the counter. Folding laundry? Frequent breaks. Cooking dinner? Leaning against the kitchen island like it was my closest relative.

By my third pregnancy, life had only become busier. Two energetic boys don’t exactly pause because Mommy’s pelvis feels like it’s supporting a small planet.

I needed meaningful support, not compression.
